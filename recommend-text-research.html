How we’ll write Facebook recommendation comments

A short research note for Rate Buddies · 20 November 2026, Australia/Perth. This is not legal advice.

You asked for suggested comment text, a Copy button, and optional details such as name, phone and a Facebook page. You also asked members to add a few of their own words, so comments do not all look the same to Facebook. This page is what the official rules say, and how the new card is designed.

What the rules say

Facebook (Meta)

  • Facebook does not allow comments meant to deceive people or to artificially boost a post. Posting the same kind of content over and over is listed as a spam warning, even if you are not doing it at a huge speed.
  • In 2025 Facebook said comments it treats as coordinated fake engagement will be shown to fewer people.
  • The app must never post the comment for the member. Each person should write from their own account, in their own words, and not all at the same moment.

Sources: Meta Spam policy · Meta, April 2025 · Inauthentic Behavior

Facebook groups

Each group has its own rules. Many community groups remove comments that are mostly a phone number, a website, or a sales pitch. We cannot know every group’s rules, so links and phone numbers stay off unless the member turns them on, and we remind them to check the group first.

Australian law (ACCC)

  • It is against the law for a business to create a fake or misleading review, or to get someone else to do it. This includes comments on social media.
  • A review should be the genuine opinion of someone who actually used the service.
  • Warning signs of a fake review include a burst of similar praise in a short time, generic comments with no real detail, and comments that use the same wording.
  • If someone is a friend, employee, or in business with the person they are recommending, they should say so. Hiding that can mislead people.
  • Paying or gifting someone for a positive review is a problem. Rate Buddies will not reward members for leaving a Facebook comment.

Sources: ACCC, online reviews · business.gov.au

Google and Nextdoor (useful lessons)

  • Google lets you ask for a genuine review, but not tell people what to write, and not offer a discount or gift in return. It also does not allow phone numbers or website links inside a Google review.
  • Nextdoor’s model is a real neighbour who has actually used the business. No paid or anonymous recommendations.

Sources: Google contribution policy · Nextdoor Faves

How the new card works

  1. Pick a starter. Each member gets three short suggestions that nobody else has been given for that same Facebook post. They can tap “More options” for a fresh set. Old ones are not reused.
  2. Add your own words. A clear box asks how they know Michael, what job he did, or one thing they liked. This is the most important step. When they add words, the card shows “Personal touch added ✓”.
  3. Add details, if they want. Name and business can stay in the comment. Phone number and links are off unless they turn them on. Only one link at a time. On My profile, a member can choose which of their details others are allowed to share.
  4. Copy and open the post. The copied text is the starter, plus their own words, plus any details they chose. The button says “Copy my recommendation”. If they have not added their own words, we warn them once, then allow a second tap. After copying, we say “Copied — now paste it on Facebook”, with a clear button to open the post. “I recommended Michael” still marks it done.

The tip at the bottom is: “Make it yours: tweak a word or two before posting. Only recommend Michael if you genuinely know his work.”

When this goes live, alerts to Perth members will be spread out over a few hours, so a dozen comments do not land in two minutes.
